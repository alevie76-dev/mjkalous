My dad rammed home his belief that I would turn out to be no good and "worthless." He spoke that curse over me and my brothers in his drunken rages, driving that cursing message home with his nail-hard finger, stabbing us in the chest. My brothers must have taken in and lived out that message, because that curse came true, for them. It did not come true for me. I had something they did not. I found hope in the midst of despair. I had the desire for something more and sought and found heroes to give me a different message.  That is a very difficult thing to find when repetitive curses of a parent, or someone else with power, wears upon a child's innocent world, collapsing their house of identity, and crumbling the walls of hope to fall in a heap on the floor of their soul. "What made you different?" I am often asked. My easiest answer was always God. When asked to do a TedxTalk I wanted a more in-depth answer. That is how I developed what I call the HERO Construct. These four things helped me overcome the curses of my childhood and brought me to a place of wisdom and hope. Here is the basic outline: 

**H**ero: Someone from whom we can borrow hope, courage, and strength. 

**E**xpression (Voice ): Some way to "speak" out our fear, hurt, despair, and sorrow. To use some vehicle, other than our words to expel the negative messages we received from our parents, etc. These vehicles could be songs and singing, poetry, dance, art, sports, and anything else that allows us to release the negative energy and have a cathartic experience, and experience positive emotions. 

**R**efuge: We must have some way to remove ourselves from the abuse, the toxic relationship, the dangerous environment; and though this may not be possible in the actual world, we must be able to do this in our mental, emotional, or spiritual realms. For me, school was a refuge; reading was a refuge, and long, solitary walks in forest or city were places of refuge. I speak of how, in my mind, when experiencing abuse, I was able to exit the physical realm, and in my mental or spiritual realm, remove myself to a place of mere observation and safety. I am not speaking of dissociation, I am speaking of not allowing my "self" to receive the harm into my soul. 

**O**utlet: One of the best ways to find hope and healing is to give to others the love that we did not receive from someone who should have loved us. When I care about another person, I am exercising the principle that good gathers good, and the Spiritual Law of Reciprocity returns good to me. I am blessed as I bless another. Think of this: Good gathers good; Evil compounds evil. We must choose which way we go.
